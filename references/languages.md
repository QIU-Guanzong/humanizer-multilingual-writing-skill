# Experimental language profiles

These profiles are starting points, not a claim of comprehensive localization or validated fluency. Apply the shared preservation rules first. Choose an explicitly requested locale and register, preserve the user's existing terms where appropriate, and seek local review for publication where wording matters. Do not mechanically convert one regional variety into another.

- **Simplified Chinese (zh-Hans):** Keep established technical and domain terms. Remove an empty scene-setting phrase when no condition is lost; keep transitions that express real cause or contrast. Avoid converting the source to Traditional Chinese without a translation request.
- **English (en):** Prefer a clear actor and verb when known; preserve passive voice when the actor is unknown or the affected party matters. Keep UK/US spelling and formal, legal or academic register as specified.
- **Japanese (ja):** Keep the specified polite/plain register and established honorifics; do not invent social relationships to select honorifics. Preserve omitted subjects when context is clear and avoid adding certainty through a rewritten ending. When the distinction matters, keep 「まだ確定していません」 rather than replacing it with 「未定」, which may suggest a different stage of decision.
- **Korean (ko):** Maintain the requested speech level and honorific relations. Do not infer age, status or intimacy; retain established technical terms and uncertainty.
- **Spanish (es):** Respect the target region, address form and existing gender choices; do not force one regional vocabulary across all Spanish-speaking audiences. Preserve explicit conditions and negations.
- **French (fr):** Respect the requested register and regional vocabulary, and retain distinctions between formal and informal address. Preserve qualifications and citations when simplifying long sentences.
- **Portuguese (pt):** Distinguish Brazilian and European usage when specified; preserve the original variant when not. Maintain technical vocabulary, register and the scope of negations and conditions.

A negative finding in one study is not a general claim that an effect does not exist. For example, keep the scope and tense of 「この結果だけで効果が証明されたわけではない」; do not replace it with a potentially broader 「効果は証明されない」. Preserve a meaningful present-time qualifier if its removal might broaden the claim.

For other languages, follow the user's language and locale, protect meaning and apply only general clarity checks. Avoid claiming a locally reviewed profile exists.
